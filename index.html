Where the numbers come from
  • Counting a set as 1 for a primary muscle and ½ for a secondary muscle ("fractional" sets) fit muscle-growth data best in two meta-regressions: Pelland et al. 2024 (weekly volume) and Remmert et al. 2025 (per session). Both are preprints, not yet peer reviewed.
  • The line at 11 sets per session is where Remmert et al. stopped detecting extra growth from more sets. It is guidance, not a limit, and the authors say to read it cautiously.
  • The line at 10 sets per week follows Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 (J Sports Sci 35:1073), where 10 or more weekly sets per muscle grew more than fewer.
  • Target = your best weight for one more rep, the rep-first progression in the ACSM 2009 position stand, which adds 2–10% load once you beat the rep goal by 1–2 reps.
  • Strength uses estimated one-rep max = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30) (Epley), from sets of 10 reps or fewer because estimates get worse above that (Mayhew et al. 2008). Each lift is compared with its own first week in the range (= 100), then lifts are averaged per primary muscle. Assisted and zero-weight lifts are left out.
  • Adding or dropping sets on the day is volume autoregulation, reviewed in Larsen et al. 2021.
  • Exercises and muscle tags: free-exercise-db (public domain), pinned at commit f00c92c.
