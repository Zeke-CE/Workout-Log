    Where the numbers come from
    • Counting a set as 1 for a primary muscle and ½ for a secondary muscle ("fractional" sets) fit muscle-growth data best in two meta-regressions: Pelland et al. 2024 (weekly volume) and Remmert et al. 2025 (per session). Both are preprints, not yet peer reviewed.
    • The line at 11 sets per session is where Remmert et al. stopped detecting extra growth from more sets. It is guidance, not a limit, and the authors say to read it cautiously.
    • The line at 10 sets per week follows Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 (J Sports Sci 35:1073), where 10 or more weekly sets per muscle grew more than fewer.
    • Target = your best weight for one more rep, the rep-first progression in the ACSM 2009 position stand, which adds 2–10% load once you beat the rep goal by 1–2 reps.
    • Strength uses estimated one-rep max = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30) (Epley), from sets of 10 reps or fewer because estimates get worse above that (Mayhew et al. 2008). Each lift is compared with its own first week in the range (= 100), then lifts are averaged per primary muscle. Assisted and zero-weight lifts are left out.
    • Adding or dropping sets on the day is volume autoregulation, reviewed in Larsen et al. 2021.
    • Intensity techniques are sub-sets logged under a set. Drop sets grew muscle about as much as straight sets in a half to a third of the time (Sødal et al. 2023, meta-analysis of 5 studies), and rest-pause matched straight sets when total volume was equal (Enes et al. 2021; Prestes et al. 2019). Myo reps are a rest-pause variant (one set near failure, then mini-sets on short rests); no trial of them by name is cited here.
    • Partial reps added after full-rep failure, done in the stretched part of the lift, grew calves more than stopping at failure (9.6% vs 6.7% in 10 weeks, Larsen et al. 2025, one study of 23 untrained men). Full range is still the default; partials do best at long muscle lengths (Wolf et al. 2023).
    • No study gives a conversion from a technique sub-set to a straight set, so sub-sets are tallied on their own (grey) with the same 1 and ½ weighting and are not added to the set totals or compared with the guide lines. Their weight × reps does count toward Load, with partial reps scaled by their share of the range. Bests, targets and Strength use straight sets only.
    • The exercise list and its muscle tags come from RepDB (next item). free-exercise-db (public domain, commit f00c92c) still tags older exercise names in your history, and it is the list shown while RepDB loads or if it can't load.
    • The exercise picker, Build's volume per muscle and Sets per muscle (the body map) use the finer muscle lists from RepDB (free tier, commit 66c4298), with the same 1 and ½ counting. The body map counts straight sets only. Tap a muscle on the map to see its sets. Your browser downloads that file from RepDB. Older names in your history are matched to RepDB by exact name (ignoring case and punctuation) or by a hand-made list of equivalents. An exercise you add is counted on the exact muscles you pick for it. One added before exact muscles could be picked only has muscle groups, so it is counted on the main muscle of each group (all three deltoid heads for shoulders) until you edit it. The muscle-group charts above put each RepDB muscle into one group. The map's color scale tops out at the busiest muscle-week in the range. The map (body-muscles 1.0.0, Apache-2.0) has no region for some muscles, so these are drawn on the nearest one and marked "approx." in the list: rotator cuff, rhomboids, brachialis, brachioradialis, hip abductors and the deep abdominal muscle. Placement follows OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology 2e, ch. 11.
